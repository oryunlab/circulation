Circulation

Cupping, Colour, and Collective Sensing

Three people each hold a small cup. Every finger lights its own colour. The light then travels inward to a shared centre, the Hap, where the three hands' colours meet and mix into one.

Join the workshop See the design
The idea

From one fingertip to a centre that belongs to nobody

Circulation takes the cupping cup, a tool of East Asian body care, and turns it into an instrument for sensing together. It works with the cultural map of the Five Elements as a way of seeing. It is not medical advice.

01 · THE CUP

Five fingers, five elements

Each cup is 7 cm across, small enough to cradle. It has five pressure pads, one under each finger. Press, and that finger's colour lights at once, close to your hand.

02 · THE HAP

A shared centre

The three cups sit in a round table. Light steps inward from each cup along a vessel of acrylic rods. At the centre, the three streams orbit, tighten as the hands agree, and fuse into one colour.

03 · TOGETHER

Distance becomes time

Your own light is instant. The centre is slow: it takes all three of you, held together for a while, to reach it. What you see in the middle is something no one made alone.

Thumb to little finger follows the generating cycle (tương sinh · 相生): Wood feeds Fire, Fire feeds Earth, Earth feeds Metal, Metal feeds Water. Closing your hand around the cup closes the cycle.

Hap · hợp · 合

Why the centre is called the Hap

Classical East Asian medicine describes each channel of the body as water: it rises at a fingertip well and flows on until it enters and joins at 合. In Korean, Vietnamese and Chinese the character means to join, to unite, to work in harmony. The installation follows the same five stages.

經

合
Hap
hợp · hé

Where it enters and joins: three people's light meets at the centre.

The five transport points, 五輸穴 (ngũ du huyệt · wǔshūxué), from the Huangdi Neijing Lingshu, chapter 1. Readings are given in Vietnamese and Mandarin.

Collective sensing

Feeling the others through the cup

The table is built so that you notice the other two people, not only your own light.

Ghost touchWhen someone presses, a faint echo of their colour shows on your own pads.
One breathThe cups and the centre glow and dim together, six times a minute, a slow shared breath.
ArrivalWhen another person's light reaches the centre, your cup gives a small buzz in your hand.
Letting go togetherRelease within a moment of each other, and the centre answers with one last bloom.
SoundA sound layer follows the light as it gathers, braids and fuses.
Workshop · November 2026

Make your own glowing cup

A two-hour hands-on workshop for students and staff of the School of Communication and Design. Try the Hap with two other people. Then build a cup of your own: a pressure sensor and a strip of light on a small board, with a clay grip shaped to your hand.

No experience with electronics is needed. Everything is provided, and you'll work in groups of three.
