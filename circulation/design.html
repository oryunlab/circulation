The Hap: from fingertip to centre

Three 7 cm cups sit in the Hap (합 · hợp · 合), a round table where three people meet. Each finger pad lights its element at once. Light then steps inward along a vessel, and the three streams orbit in a field of acrylic rods. The orbit tightens as the three hands come into agreement, then fuses at the core. Press the sliders, play the demo, and turn the sound on.

Hap · 합 · hợp · 合

The table at the centre of Circulation is called the Hap. 合 is the last of the five transport points (五輸穴), the "sea" point where a channel's flow enters and joins. The character means to join and unite in all three languages: Korean 합 (hap, as in 화합, harmony), Vietnamese hợp (as in hợp tác, to cooperate) and Chinese 合 (hé, as in 合作). Circulation follows the same five stages, from fingertip to centre.

The centre: Sea and Peak, with a bowl of water

The centre of the Hap is changing from the field of acrylic rods (drawn further down) to Sea and Peak, after the sea-water and cliff pattern (海水江崖紋) at the hem of East Asian court robes, where streams run into rolling waves and a rock rises from the sea. Here the three vessels are the rivers (江), two low wave rings around the centre are the sea (海), and a stone at the centre is the peak (崖). On the peak sits a bowl of water, jeonghwasu (정화수): the first water drawn from the well at dawn and set out for the people one cares for. This is 合, where the three meet.

Each cup has five finger pads, and how a person presses mixes the five element colours into their own colour. When the three hands press in unalike ways, the light on the waves is choppy, each colour on its own side. As the hands come into tune, the sea calms, the colours blend, and the light rises to the bowl: ripples cross the water, and the water throws moving patterns onto the ceiling. Participants hold the cups and never touch the water.

Sensing together

Each sense has a part you get alone and a part that only exists when two or three people hold at once. Sight, sound, touch and breath all carry the same message: the others are here, and you are moving toward each other.

Waiting at the shore

One person alone sees their light travel inward and then circle the outer rods, unable to go further. The centre opens only when a second hand arrives. This invites strangers to join.in sim

Same finger, same moment

When all three press hardest with the same finger, that element washes the whole table for a few seconds. It's a call people discover together and then repeat on purpose. Try "Still water".in sim

Letting go together

When all three release within about two seconds of each other, the centre makes one last slow bloom and fades. That glow is the session's mark, like the afterglow on each cup.not in sim

Fixed for sound

The interaction rules: what agreement means, when the flood and the closing bloom happen, and the 10 s shared breath. Sound should reinforce these moments, not add new ones.

Patrick decides

Tuning and timbre. Whether the five-tone scale stays. One speaker in the light box or one per cup. Whether the out-of-tune beating idea is kept.

Agree together

Volume ceiling for a quiet room, and what plays when nobody is holding. The sim uses silence.

Earlier centre: the rod field, exploded

The light box is a 26 cm drum set into the table, with the lid flush to the tabletop. It has five layers, top to bottom. The rods drop into sockets in the lid, pass through a black baffle, and stop just above their own pixel. The rod colours show a moment of medium agreement: three strands orbiting on the middle ring.

Socket and mounting details

Left: one rod socket at 6× scale, which is where most of the light is won or lost. Right: a half-section through the table edge showing how the drum hangs from below and the vessel enters it.

Section through cups A and B

Drawn to scale. Rods rise toward the centre, so the shared light is the highest point but stays below a seated person's eye line. The three people can still see each other across the table.

Cup pad band, unrolled

Ø 7 cm gives a 22.0 cm circumference, so five pads sit at a 4.4 cm pitch. The thumb-to-little-finger order follows the generating cycle. A clear 3 mm rod in each groove carries the pad's light down to the table.

Pad

About 2.2 × 2.6 cm oval, one FSR behind each. One NeoPixel just behind the pad lights both the pad and the top of its groove rod.

Groove rod

3 mm clear acrylic, sanded so it glows along its length. Pad ring printed in opaque black PETG so the five channels don't bleed into each other.

Dome

White or translucent print, lit by a 7-LED jewel. Shows the hand's blended colour and breathes at 6 per minute. A coin vibration motor sits under the dome.

Design notes and open questions

  • ChangedThe table is 100 cm, not 75–80 cm. With 7 cm cups seated 10 cm from the edge and a 26 cm light box, a smaller table leaves vessels only about 8 cm long, which is too short to see light travel. At 100 cm each vessel is about 23 cm.
  • ChangedOne loose pixel under each rod (cut WS2812B strip or single-pixel boards) instead of a concentric ring set. Off-the-shelf ring sets top out near 10 cm across, too small for a 22 cm rod circle.
  • No pulseAgreement here comes from how alike the three hands' five-finger patterns are, plus a bonus when the dominant elements form a generating chain. On hardware I'd add a third term: how closely the squeeze rhythms match the shared breath.
  • NeedThe cup's height from the CAD. The section assumes 7.5 cm.
  • DecideSeated (table 74 cm) or standing (90–95 cm). The section assumes seated, with eyes about 44 cm above the tabletop.
