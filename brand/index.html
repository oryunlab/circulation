How to read the mark

Three layers

The five is already in the shape and in the name, so colour is free to say something else.

五

Form: five

Five petals: five people, five elements, five senses. The name 오륜 begins with ㅇ, itself a circle.

輪 · 倫

Line: circle and bond

Oryun holds two readings, 五輪 five rings and 五倫 five rules. The knot is one unbroken line: to get from any petal to the next it must pass through the shared centre. The orbit around it is the community that holds it.

間

Colour: between

Not the five themselves, but what they become when they meet. Each level of the work shows a different between.

Brand structure

Studio, programme, project

The same knot at every level. Colour tells you which level you are looking at. In each chip the large dot is the night colour and the small dot its paper version.

The studio

Oryun Lab오륜

Twilight in 간색

녹 청 + 황 · Wood 홍 적 + 백 · Fire 유황 황 + 흑 · Earth 벽 청 + 백 · Metal 자 적 + 흑 · Water

Korean colour tradition pairs the five 정색 with five 간색, in-between colours made by mixing them. Each petal keeps its own element and leans toward its neighbour. Five colours of meeting, for a studio about what happens between people. The ring is the Depth ring: ink, thin at the back and full in front.

Used on studio pages, stationery, avatars, anything signed by the studio.

The research programme

Circulatory Sensing

Where Fire meets Water

적 Fire 자 Fire + Water 흑 Water

The knot in ink; colour only in the ring, flowing Fire to 자 to Water and back. In Eastern thought fire rises and water sinks, and their meeting is balance: the I Ching image 既濟 (63) is water above fire. Purple appears only where they meet.

Used on research outputs, talks, papers and this website.

Use

A few rules

  • Night first. The marks are made for dark grounds; use the paper versions only on print and light documents.
  • One level at a time. A page or object carries the mark of the level it belongs to: studio, programme or project.
  • Small sizes (under about 64 px) use the small-size files: heavier lines, no fine cuts.
  • Never recolour a single petal, stretch the mark, or remove the orbit from the full mark.
  • The Five Elements are used as a cultural coordinate system, not as medical advice.
